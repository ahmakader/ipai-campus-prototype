MODEL TRANSPARENCY

A campus you can question.

This independent learning prototype adapts the prompting mechanics described in Arpan Bakshi’s article to the IPAI campus. It is not an official IPAI product or an engineering digital twin.

What transfers from the article?

Build in 3D through prompt cards; distinguish what was specified, derived and assumed; predict a result; run a fair test; inspect trade-offs for people and the environment; revise the instruction. Like the original, this guide uses deterministic responses populated by model calculations. No model service, account or API key is needed.

Your supplied campus model

The 3D geometry comes from FullCampus.glb. Original building coordinates, orientation and scale are retained; a uniform 180 m vertical offset places the campus near the viewer origin. The procedural reconstruction has been replaced.

The web copy reduces 35.52 million triangles to 7.53 million and 1.86 GB to 108.91 MB with mesh simplification and Meshopt compression. The simplifier uses a 3.5 cm error threshold plus 1 mm coordinate rounding; this is an algorithmic tolerance, not a certified survey deviation. 130 off-site duplicate assembly meshes are excluded. The original file is unchanged.

Materials: 18 texture images from the supplied project folder are used for timber, concrete, paving, vegetation, Solarix and solar cells. The original GLB has no UV coordinates or texture links. Assignments were reconstructed from building folders and object names, using world-space triplanar mapping at assumed scales. This does not reproduce the original 3ds Max shader graphs or exact mapping. Glass and metal use reconstructed physical properties and a procedural reflection environment. Unmapped usable GLB colours are preserved; named black fallback materials remain neutral. Project textures is the default view. The Project textures button restores this view from any analysis layer. Clickable Energy, Water and Shade hotspots connect places to teaching interventions. Solar panels retain their supplied geometry and positions. Gold overlays on existing surfaces show an illustrative active-capacity fraction; battery blocks respond to capacity controls. Other scenario additions are teaching overlays. The six published profiles below are reference information and may differ from the supplied design revision.

Exploration & graphics

WebGPU rendering is used when available, with a WebGL2 fallback shown in the view. Rain uses 8,192 compute-shader particles on WebGPU and 1,024 instanced particles on the fallback. Rain is visual weather, not a fluid solver. Section cuts reveal the supplied geometry and do not create section caps. The solar layer shows panel surface orientation relative to the illustrative sun; it excludes occlusion and is not an irradiance or energy-yield analysis. Building-use colours follow the six mapped building assemblies; other objects are context.

How the tests work

Energy: a 24-hour, 15-minute-step balance. PV area × 0.20 kWp/m² × an illustrative daily solar profile. A simplified occupancy-sensitive demand profile serves all six buildings. Storage starts empty, charges only from excess PV and discharges with 90% round-trip efficiency. Grid supply always covers remaining demand. Roof area uses the published rectangular dimensions and an assumed 60% usable fraction, not measured roof surfaces. Roof PV coverage, battery capacity and occupancy are training assumptions, not installed equipment.

Rainwater: a one-hour water balance over a fixed 12,000 m² teaching catchment. Rainfall × area is split between surface storage/infiltration and runoff with assumed coefficients of 0.90 (hard surface) and 0.25 (permeable). An initially empty tank stores runoff up to its capacity. Overflow is exported runoff, not a prediction of flood depth or a certified drainage design.

Public space: A distributed set of open-campus ground samples test shadow intersections against approximate building bounding envelopes and the added tree/canopy geometry at 14:00 on an illustrative summer day. Added trees and a canopy affect the sampled shade. This is a shade-access proxy, not thermal comfort, wind analysis or a heat-stress calculation. Tree irrigation uses an illustrative 35 litres/tree/day.

Campus in motion

Six illustrative live scenarios add crowd routing and queues, surface-temperature and shade studies with tree placement, drifting clouds and battery dispatch, conservative grid-based rainfall redistribution, delivery-robot routing and charging, and a hypothetical waste-heat network. Each panel states its model assumptions. These scenarios are separate from the original prompt-card exercises. Sampled terrain is incomplete; no live operational feeds or calibrated engineering models are connected.

KBP energy sharing

The seventh scenario is a simplified steady-state teaching model informed by KBP Ingenieure, IPAI Digitaler Zwilling | Anergienetz, September 2026, slides 3–10. It explores simultaneous heating/cooling, Kaufland waste heat, geothermal balancing and source outages. Routes, loads, equipment capacities and COP curves are assumptions; it is not KBP’s hydraulic solver or live digital twin.
